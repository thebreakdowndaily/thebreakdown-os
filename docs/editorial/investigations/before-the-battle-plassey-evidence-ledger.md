## 1. Investigative Scope & Historical Questions

The conventional retelling of the Battle of Plassey (23 June 1757) portrays it as a military triumph of European discipline and tactical brilliance over a decadent Asian army outnumbering them nearly twenty to one.

This investigation tests that narrative against primary archival records, contemporary Persian chronicles, and modern economic and social historiography. It examines:

1. **Commercial & Fiscal Origins:** How did East India Company (EIC) abuse of imperial *dastaks* (duty-free trade permits) and illegal private trade undermine the fiscal sovereignty of the Bengal Subah?
2. **The Banking Core:** What was the structural role of the House of Jagat Seth—the subcontinent's premier financiers—in orchestrating and underwriting the regime change?
3. **The Pre-Battle Coalition:** How did political alienation, internal military factionalism, and commercial panic unite Robert Clive, Mir Jafar, Rai Durlabh, and the Jagat Seths into an offensive alliance before a single shot was fired?
4. **Tactical Contingencies vs. Premeditation:** What occurred on the field of Palashi on 23 June 1757, particularly the midday monsoon downpour, the tarpaulin-protected gunpowder, the death of Mir Madan, and the deliberate paralysis of 35,000 troops under Mir Jafar and Rai Durlabh?
5. **Historiographical Synthesis:** Was Plassey a military victory, an imperial conquest, or a commercial transaction and palace coup financed by Indian capital?

## 3. Disputed Claims & Historiographical Uncertainties

1. **The Physical Slap of Jagat Seth:**
   - *Claim:* Siraj slapped Seth Mahtab Rai in open durbar.
   - *Status:* Disputed / Anecdotal. Chronicled in *Siyar-ul-Mutakhkherin*, but omitted in contemporary English letters.
   - *Editorial Decision:* Present as a chronicled reflection of the intense hostility and mutual terror between the Nawab and the bankers, without asserting it as an uncontested physical fact.
2. **The Black Hole Casualties:**
   - *Claim:* 123 of 146 British prisoners died in the Black Hole of Calcutta.
   - *Status:* Disproved colonial propaganda. Modern consensus (Gupta, Little, Marshall) indicates ~64 total prisoners and 43 casualties from heat asphyxiation.
   - *Editorial Decision:* Include with explicit historiographical debunking, highlighting how exaggerated casualties were weaponized to justify the subsequent coup.
3. **The Inevitability of British Dominance:**
   - *Claim:* Plassey made British rule over India inevitable.
   - *Status:* False retrospective determinism. The EIC position remained precarious until the Battle of Buxar (1764) and the Diwani grant of 1765; Mir Jafar was later deposed for Mir Qasim, who fought back fiercely.
   - *Editorial Decision:* Explicitly reject inevitable imperial conquest; characterize Plassey as an unstable fiscal-mercenary coup whose full imperial consequences took decades to unfold.
