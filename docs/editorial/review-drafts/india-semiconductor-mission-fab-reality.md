# Review draft — India's semiconductor mission: approval is not output

**Status:** Editorial review required; not approved for publication.  
**Working thesis:** India's semiconductor programme has moved beyond announcements: government records now report five plants in commercial production. But the headline number of approved projects combines different parts of the value chain, so it cannot by itself establish that India has achieved self-sufficiency in advanced silicon wafer fabrication.  
**Target:** 2,000–2,800 narrative words.  
**Current production baseline:** 304 narrative words; 5 paragraphs; declared 11-minute read.

## Proposed opening

A semiconductor plant can be real, operational and economically useful without doing the same job as a silicon wafer fab. Packaging and testing facilities prepare chips for use; a wafer fab creates integrated circuits on silicon wafers. Both matter to the supply chain, but they are different industrial capabilities, with different equipment, process requirements, customers and technical risks.

That distinction is essential to reading India's semiconductor announcements. The latest government figures show a programme progressing from approvals to production. They do not mean that every approved facility is operational, that all plants manufacture the same kind of chip, or that the country has already eliminated its dependence on imported semiconductors.

## What the latest official numbers say

The Press Information Bureau's September 17, 2026 update reports 12 approved semiconductor manufacturing units and cumulative investment exceeding ₹1.64 lakh crore. It breaks the portfolio into one silicon fab, one silicon-carbide fab, one gallium-nitride micro-LED display fab and nine ATMP/OSAT packaging units. The same update reports that five units had commenced commercial production. A separate September 18, 2026 PIB release likewise says five plants had begun commercial production.

These are different measures: project approvals, planned investment and production status. They should not be collapsed into one claim of output or self-sufficiency. The approval total is a count of projects, not a count of advanced silicon wafer fabs.

The Cabinet approved Semicon 2.0 in July 2026 with an outlay of ₹1,27,500 crore, extending the programme beyond the first phase's ₹76,000 crore outlay. Official descriptions say the second phase broadens attention to design, equipment and materials, more fabs, advanced packaging, research and development, and talent. The policy rationale is to develop a wider ecosystem rather than a single factory type.

## Reporting sequence

1. **Build a dated project table.** For each approved unit, record owner/partners, location, technology, project category, investment commitment, approval date, announced milestones, construction status, commissioning status and whether commercial production has been independently reported. Each field needs a dated source.
2. **Separate fabrication from packaging.** Explain what silicon fabs, compound-semiconductor fabs, display fabs and ATMP/OSAT plants do. Avoid implying that packaging is inferior; it is simply a different stage and capability.
3. **Test the word “commercial.”** Find what the company or government says is being produced, for which applications, at what stage and when. Do not infer yields, volumes, customer qualification or profitability from the phrase “commercial production” unless evidence establishes them.
4. **Follow the money.** Distinguish approved investment, committed investment, disbursed subsidy, private capital expenditure and actual expenditure. Do not treat the ₹1.64 lakh crore commitment as money already spent.
5. **Map dependencies.** Investigate equipment, specialty gases, chemicals, water, electricity, workforce and supplier qualifications. Support claims with project documents and named experts, not generic assertions about “ecosystem gaps.”
6. **Explain the economics.** Report capex, process node, utilization, yields, customer commitments and depreciation only where company filings or reliable named sources support them. Do not repeat unsupported margin or value-addition percentages.
7. **Examine the counterargument.** Packaging capacity and mature-node or compound semiconductor production can create jobs, learning and useful domestic capability before a leading-edge fab is operational. Evaluate that contribution on its own terms.
8. **Compare with the global chain.** Explain why semiconductor production is internationally specialized and why domestic capacity does not automatically equal self-sufficiency. Use comparable, dated data.
9. **Conclusion.** The meaningful test is not the number of ribbon-cuttings; it is which capabilities operate, at what scale, with what products and customers, and how much value the evidence shows is being created domestically.

## Editorial critique before revision

The article must avoid both promotional overstatement and reflexive dismissal. Approval counts are meaningful but do not demonstrate output or profitability. Commercial production matters but must be broken down by facility and process. The next pass needs a verified project-level dataset, industry-side counterevidence, and careful treatment of capex, yield, customers and domestic value addition.
