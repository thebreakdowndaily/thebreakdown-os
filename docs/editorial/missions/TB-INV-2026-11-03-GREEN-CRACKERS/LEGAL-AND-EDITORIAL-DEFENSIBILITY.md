## 1. Legal Accuracy and Statutory Review

### Case Details & Jurisdiction
- **Court:** Supreme Court of India
- **Cause Title:** *Arjun Gopal and Others v. Union of India and Others*
- **Citation:** 2026 INSC 1181
- **Order Date:** 29 October 2026 (digitally signed 2 November 2026)
- **Jurisdiction:** National Capital Region (NCR) encompassing NCT of Delhi and designated districts of Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, and Rajasthan.

### Legal Verification Checklist:
1. **Window Segmentation:**
   - *Manufacture:* 2–6 November 2026 (Direction C). Accurately distinguished from sale window.
   - *Sale:* 4–6 November 2026 (Direction G). Accurately distinguished from bursting window.
   - *Bursting:* 7–8 November 2026, 6:00–7:00 am and 8:00–10:00 pm (Direction A). Total 3 hours per day.
   - *Prohibition Re-imposition:* 9 November 2026 onwards (Direction A).
   - *Defensibility Verdict:* **FULLY DEFENDED.**

2. **Substantive Product Safeguards:**
   - Must be CSIR-NEERI and PESO certified.
   - Must appear on the registered list.
   - Must be in sealed packaging.
   - Must carry a product-specific and batch-specific non-transferable QR code (Direction F).
   - Barium salts and joined crackers strictly banned.
   - Online sales strictly banned.
   - *Defensibility Verdict:* **FULLY DEFENDED.**

3. **Judicial Findings vs. Observations:**
   - The Court's observation on 3 November regarding the *Hindustan Times* report was a directive to the Union, Delhi Government, and Police to "look into" the allegations. It does NOT constitute a final judicial determination of guilt or statutory violation by individual vendors. The article rigorously avoids framing judicial notice as a legal finding of guilt.
   - The Court's finding in its 29 October order that authorities failed to provide satisfactory implementation data regarding the 15 October 2025 relaxation is directly quoted from paragraph 4 of the order.
   - *Defensibility Verdict:* **FULLY DEFENDED.**
