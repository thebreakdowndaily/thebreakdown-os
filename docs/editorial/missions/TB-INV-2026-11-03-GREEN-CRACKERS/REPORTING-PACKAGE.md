## Editorial question

Can Delhi-NCR authorities demonstrate that the Supreme Court's temporary green-cracker framework was implemented as ordered, and can they produce scientifically interpretable evidence about environmental and health effects?

This is an implementation and evidence-accountability investigation. It is not a claim that green crackers are pollution-free, that all vendors are violating the law, or that any observed AQI movement was caused by fireworks.

## Critical date correction

The 29 October 2026 Supreme Court order distinguishes three windows:

- **Manufacture of approved products:** 2–6 November 2026.
- **Sale by eligible traders:** 4–6 November 2026.
- **Bursting:** 7–8 November 2026, only 6:00–7:00 am and 8:00–10:00 pm.
- **Complete prohibition restored:** 9 November 2026.

Do not state that sale was permitted from 2 November. The earlier desk draft conflated the manufacturing and sale windows. The order was digitally signed on 2 November, although dated 29 October.

## Defensible central finding at research cut-off

The Supreme Court has issued a detailed, temporary compliance framework. Its 29 October order also records that authorities had not supplied material satisfactory to the Court about implementation of the 15 October 2025 experiment. On 3 November 2026, the Court took note of a Hindustan Times market report alleging the availability of conventional and unauthorised products, and asked the Union, Delhi government and police to look into it.

The market allegations remain attributed reporting, not independent findings by The Breakdown. No independent field visit, QR scan, licence check, official enforcement dataset or hospital dataset has been completed by this desk.

## Article draft

# Delhi's Green-Cracker Test Is Now an Enforcement Test

*The Supreme Court has permitted a limited sale and use window for certified green crackers in Delhi-NCR. But after the Court questioned the lack of implementation evidence from the previous experiment and took note of fresh market-sale allegations, the central question is whether authorities can verify products, enforce restrictions and publish usable environmental and health data.*

**By The Breakdown Research Desk**

Delhi-NCR's latest green-cracker experiment is being tested before the first permitted fireworks are burst. The immediate question is not simply whether certified products are available. It is whether authorities can demonstrate that the products being sold are the products the regulatory system has approved.

On 29 October 2026, the Supreme Court permitted a tightly limited return of certified green crackers in Delhi-NCR. The order allows manufacture of approved products from 2 to 6 November, sale by eligible traders from 4 to 6 November, and bursting only on 7 and 8 November between 6 am and 7 am and 8 pm and 10 pm. The complete prohibition is to return from 9 November.

The distinction between those dates matters. The manufacturing window is not the sale window, and neither is permission to burst crackers. The order also prohibits bursting within 300 metres of specified hospitals, health centres, nursing homes, old-age homes, notified silence zones and educational institutions.

The conditions are specific. Products must be certified by CSIR-NEERI and PESO, appear on the relevant NEERI list, be sold in sealed boxes and carry product-specific, non-transferable QR codes. The QR code is required to show the manufacturer, NEERI registration, PESO or District Magistrate licence, formulation and test certificate. Barium-containing crackers, unapproved products and joined-series crackers are prohibited. Online sales are not permitted.

But the Court's order is not proof that these conditions have been implemented.

### A court order cannot verify a box on a shop counter

On 3 November, the Supreme Court took note of a Hindustan Times report describing conventional and allegedly unauthorised firecrackers being offered in several Delhi markets. The report said some products lacked QR codes, while some codes reportedly led to catalogues rather than product-specific certification information. It also described alleged advance orders and concealed stocks.

The Court asked the Centre, Delhi government and police to look into the report.

Those observations should be treated carefully. The market findings are reported by Hindustan Times; The Breakdown has not independently visited those markets, purchased or inspected products, or scanned any QR code. The Court's reference to the report establishes that the allegations were brought to its attention, not that every allegation has been judicially proved.

The allegations nevertheless point to a verifiable regulatory question: can a consumer, trader or enforcement officer establish that a particular sealed box and batch correspond to an authentic certification record?

CSIR-NEERI says that registration certificates and test reports issued through its official online system are the only authentic and valid documents. Its published guidance describes registration as product-specific and linked to manufacturers holding the relevant PESO or District Magistrate licence. That makes matching the product, manufacturer, registration and batch more important than merely finding a QR symbol on a package.

A QR code is a pointer, not proof by itself. Verification requires checking where it resolves, whether the record is authentic, whether the product and batch match, and whether the underlying registration and licence are valid.

### What the rules require authorities to do

The Supreme Court directed District Collectors or Commissioners, in consultation with district police leadership, to identify designated sales locations. Authorities must prevent sales elsewhere, check vehicles entering Delhi-NCR, and put up public information explaining how consumers can identify approved products and report violations.

Police and district administration teams are required to patrol designated sales locations, familiarise themselves with NEERI's product information, conduct random QR-code checks, confiscate illegal or conventional crackers and initiate prosecutions against offenders. Manufacturers and traders are also required to furnish monetary security, which may be forfeited for violations.

These directions create a chain of accountability: certification, licensing, designated sale, inspection, seizure and prosecution. A breakdown at any point can weaken the system.

The public should be able to see the basic evidence behind each stage: district notifications naming sale locations; lists of temporarily restored trader licences; inspection dates and locations; counts of QR checks; product samples sent for analysis; seizures; prosecutions; and security deposits forfeited.

The order sets out responsibilities. Whether the public record will show that they were carried out is a separate question.

### The previous experiment left an evidence gap

The 2026 order reviews the earlier regulatory history. The Court says the temporary relaxation in October 2025 was intended, in part, to help States determine the environmental and health effects of allowing green crackers and place their findings before the Court. It records that the required material was not provided to the Court's satisfaction.

That is more than a procedural detail. It means the new experiment is being conducted against a record in which the previous exercise did not produce the evidence the Court expected.

The Court also records its concern that conventional firecrackers continued to be sold under the guise of green crackers despite a complete ban. That observation is part of the Court's reasoning for regulating certified products temporarily rather than relying only on a blanket prohibition.

Two questions must therefore remain separate. First, were the restrictions enforced? Second, what were the environmental and health effects of the permitted use? Evidence of weak enforcement does not prove that certified crackers are safe. Equally, a change in air quality after Diwali does not, by itself, establish the share attributable to fireworks.

### “Green” does not mean pollution-free

CSIR-NEERI's published definition describes green crackers as products designed to reduce emissions compared with conventional compositions in the same category. Its criteria include a minimum particulate-matter reduction of 30%, or a minimum 20% particulate-matter reduction together with at least 10% reduction in gaseous emissions, based on the specified comparison.

The comparison matters. A reduction against a conventional product is not zero emissions. NEERI's own explanation identifies particulate matter and gases such as sulphur dioxide and nitrogen dioxide among pollutants associated with fireworks.

The label should therefore not be translated into “pollution-free” or “harmless”. A certification claim is a comparative technical claim under a defined testing framework. The test report, product category, formulation and batch are needed to understand what was measured.

### Air-quality monitoring needs transparent methods

The Court directed the Central Pollution Control Board, in consultation with State Pollution Control Boards and their regional offices in NCR, to prepare daily AQI reports for three periods: 2–6 November before Diwali, 7–8 November during the permitted period, and 9–30 November afterwards. It also directed environmental sampling of sand and water.

A simple before-and-after AQI comparison cannot isolate the contribution of fireworks. Delhi-NCR's air quality is influenced by multiple sources and by meteorological conditions, including wind, temperature, atmospheric mixing and regional transport.

The Delhi government's AQI explainer says the hourly Delhi AQI is calculated from the average of stations reporting in that hour; stations without a reading in the preceding two hours are excluded from the average and marked as not reporting. The portal also distinguishes the hour a reading represents from the time the site last refreshed, and warns that real-time values are provisional pending CPCB quality checks.

That methodology makes station-level completeness important. A credible assessment should publish the stations reporting, missing observations, pollutant concentrations, lead pollutant, time stamps, quality-control status and weather context. It should distinguish the city-wide hourly index from a single station's reading.

The order also requires aggregate hospital-admission information through 30 November. Those figures can help describe health-service use, but admission counts alone cannot prove that firecrackers caused a change. A sound analysis needs a comparable baseline, consistent hospital coverage, relevant clinical categories and consideration of other seasonal and environmental factors.

### What the public should be able to verify

The Court has required the relevant reports and affidavits to be filed by 14 December 2026, with further compliance proceedings listed for 16 December.

Before that review, the public should be able to answer practical questions. Which locations were designated for sales? How many trader licences were temporarily restored? How many inspections and QR checks took place? How many products were sampled, seized or referred for testing? How many prosecutions were initiated? Which AQI stations reported complete data? Where were environmental samples collected, and what did the laboratories find? How were hospital admissions counted and compared with baseline periods?

These are not administrative footnotes. They determine whether the experiment can be evaluated independently.

The test of the green-cracker framework is not whether a product carries a green label or whether a temporary permission exists. It is whether authorities can verify what is sold, enforce what is prohibited and publish evidence that allows the public to assess what happened afterwards.

Until those records are disclosed and independently examined, neither effective enforcement nor the environmental effectiveness of the experiment should be assumed.

**The real test of the green-cracker experiment is the evidence authorities can produce.**

## Field reporting and official-record request plan

### Immediate written questions

**Delhi Police / district police**
1. Number and locations of designated sales sites.
2. Number of patrol teams and deployment dates/times.
3. Inspections conducted, shops checked and QR codes scanned.
4. Number of violations, seizures, FIRs/prosecutions and case status.
5. Complaints received and disposal status.

**Delhi Government / District Magistrates**
1. Copies of designation notifications and maps.
2. List of traders whose licences were restored, with validity dates.
3. Security deposit amount, collection and forfeiture records.
4. No-cracker-zone notifications and marking/compliance records.
5. Market inspection reports and inter-agency coordination records.

**CSIR-NEERI**
1. Dated list of approved manufacturers and product categories applicable to the 2026 order.
2. Public verification method for product-specific QR codes and batch records.
3. How consumers and enforcement teams can distinguish authentic QR codes from catalogue links or copied codes.
4. Whether the public can verify registration status and test certificates by product/batch.
5. Clarification of what the published emission-reduction criteria do and do not establish.

**PESO**
1. Publicly verifiable licence lookup process.
2. Relevant manufacturer licences and status-check procedure.
3. Product sampling and testing referral process under the Court's directions.

**CPCB / DPCC / CAQM**
1. Station-wise hourly AQI and pollutant data for 2–30 November.
2. Station uptime, missing observations, calibration/quality-control flags.
3. Sampling locations, collection protocols, lab names, analytes and results for sand/water samples.
4. Reports filed pursuant to the Supreme Court's directions.
5. Weather data used for interpretation.

**Hospitals / health authorities**
1. Aggregate daily emergency visits and admissions by relevant respiratory/irritant categories, subject to privacy safeguards.
2. Facility coverage and baseline comparison periods.
3. Coding definitions, exclusions, and any change in reporting practice.
4. Whether data are provisional or audited.

### Safe field protocol

- Do not buy, handle, transport, open or ignite fireworks for verification.
- Photograph only from lawful public vantage points and avoid identifying private individuals unnecessarily.
- Record date, time, location, product packaging and the exact observation.
- Do not state that a product is illegal based only on a missing or non-working QR code; seek regulator/manufacturer confirmation.
- A QR scan is not conclusive unless the destination and product/batch match are independently validated.
- Keep original photos, notes, URLs, timestamps and source correspondence.
- Seek responses from every named authority before publication and include material denials or explanations.
