## Article Summary
*The Supreme Court has permitted a strictly limited sale and bursting window for certified green crackers in Delhi-NCR for Diwali 2026. But following the Court's observation that authorities failed to provide satisfactory implementation data from the previous year's relaxation, and fresh allegations of unapproved market sales, the central test is whether regulatory agencies can verify products, police prohibited sales, and produce scientifically credible environmental and public-health evidence.*

---

## The Core Question

Delhi-NCR's latest regulatory experiment with green firecrackers is facing its decisive test before the first permitted firework is ignited. The immediate policy question is not merely whether certified products exist in laboratories or factory warehouses. It is whether regulatory authorities and police can demonstrate that the products sold on city streets match approved formulations, that strict territorial and temporal limits are enforced, and that the environmental and public-health consequences of the relaxation are measured transparently.

On 29 October 2026, the Supreme Court of India delivered a detailed order in *Arjun Gopal and Others v. Union of India and Others* (2026 INSC 1181), permitting a temporary, conditional return of certified green crackers across the National Capital Region. The order carefully segments the regulatory calendar:
- **Manufacture of approved products:** Permitted strictly from 2 November to 6 November 2026.
- **Sale by eligible traders:** Permitted strictly from 4 November to 6 November 2026 at designated locations.
- **Bursting of approved crackers:** Permitted strictly on 7 November and 8 November 2026, restricted to two specific daily time slots: 6:00 am to 7:00 am in the morning, and 8:00 pm to 10:00 pm in the evening.
- **Complete prohibition restored:** Takes effect automatically across Delhi-NCR from 9 November 2026 onwards.

The distinction between these dates is not an administrative formality. The manufacturing window is entirely separate from the retail sale window, and neither window permits the premature ignition of fireworks. Furthermore, the Court established strict buffer zones, completely prohibiting the bursting of firecrackers within 300 metres of hospitals, primary health centres, nursing homes, old age homes, notified silence zones, and educational institutions.

The Court's prescribed safeguards are exacting. Every permitted firework must be certified jointly by the Council of Scientific and Industrial Research–National Environmental Engineering Research Institute (CSIR-NEERI) and the Petroleum and Explosives Safety Organisation (PESO). Products must appear on NEERI's registered manufacturer schedule, be sold exclusively in tamper-evident sealed packaging, and carry a product-specific, batch-specific, non-transferable Quick Response (QR) code. That QR code is mandated to expose the manufacturer's identity, NEERI registration number, PESO or District Magistrate license details, chemical formulation type, and laboratory test certificate. All firecrackers containing barium salts, unapproved chemical compositions, and joined-series firecrackers—commonly known as "ladis"—remain strictly banned. Online sales and interstate courier deliveries into Delhi-NCR are completely prohibited.

Yet, as regulatory history in the National Capital Region has repeatedly demonstrated, the issuance of a comprehensive judicial order does not automatically translate into verified compliance on the ground.

## What Field Reporting Has Documented—and What It Cannot Prove

On 3 November 2026, the implementation challenge moved directly into the courtroom. The Supreme Court took formal judicial notice of an investigative report published by the *Hindustan Times*, which alleged that conventional and unauthorized firecrackers were being displayed and sold openly across several major commercial markets in Delhi ahead of the permitted retail window. The newspaper reported that its journalists visited five prominent retail hubs—Chandni Chowk, Sadar Bazaar, Shastri Nagar, Sarojini Nagar, and New Friends Colony—on 22 October and 2 November.

According to the *Hindustan Times* reporting, vendors were observed displaying firecracker boxes prior to the 4 November retail opening. Several packaging boxes reportedly carried QR codes that could not be resolved by smartphone cameras or redirected users to general manufacturer promotional websites rather than specific NEERI and PESO certification test sheets. The report further alleged that certain traders offered conventional firecrackers on request and accepted advance reservations for banned stock. 

Following these submissions, the Supreme Court asked the Union Government, the Government of National Capital Territory of Delhi (GNCTD), and the Delhi Police to look into the reported sales.

These allegations require careful evidentiary contextualization. The market observations and vendor conversations are third-party journalistic findings reported by the *Hindustan Times*; The Breakdown has not independently replicated those undercover market visits, purchased retail products, or scanned packaging in those markets. Crucially, the Supreme Court's decision to ask authorities to look into a media report constitutes judicial notice of an urgent regulatory concern; it is not a judicial finding of fact that every vendor in those markets violated the law. 

Nevertheless, the allegations highlight the central structural vulnerability of the green-cracker framework: can ordinary consumers, licensed traders, and beat police officers independently establish whether a sealed cardboard box on a shop counter corresponds to an authentic, authorized chemical batch?

## The Enforcement Dilemma: Police Seizures and Practical Realities

The public record does not show complete administrative inaction. Rather, it reveals substantial operational police activity operating alongside acknowledged physical limits.

In a companion report published on 3 November, the *Hindustan Times* quoted senior Delhi Police officers outlining extensive enforcement measures across several police districts:
- The Deputy Commissioner of Police (North District) reported that district teams had registered more than 13 First Information Reports (FIRs) and seized approximately 1,200 kilograms of illegal firecrackers over the preceding 12 days.
- The Deputy Commissioner of Police (Outer North District) reported the seizure of 2,878 kilograms of firecrackers between 1 October and 2 November, resulting in four criminal cases and eight arrests.
- The Deputy Commissioner of Police (East District) reported seven registered cases and the recovery of more than 1,700 kilograms of banned stock.

Delhi Police's public press-release portal further corroborates active interdiction operations. The portal's official index lists two major seizures dated 15 October 2026: a Crime Branch operation recovering 2,260 kilograms of illegal firecrackers, and an Outer North District Anti-Auto Theft Squad (AATS) operation recovering 338.5 kilograms.

These figures establish that police units have conducted targeted raids and intercepted substantial quantities of prohibited fireworks. However, these district-level figures cannot simply be aggregated into a single Delhi-wide enforcement total. The reporting windows vary widely—spanning from a single day's operation to a 12-day window or a full calendar month—and the operational jurisdictions between specialized units like the Crime Branch and local police districts may overlap. Combining them without reconciled incident registers risks double-counting and creates an illusion of false mathematical precision.

More fundamentally, seizure quantities alone cannot answer the question of enforcement efficacy. When police seize 6,000 kilograms of firecrackers, does that represent ten percent of the illegal contraband circulating within the metropolitan area, or eighty percent? Without knowing the total volume of illegal stock entering the capital across its dozens of porous regional borders, raw seizure weights cannot establish deterrence.

Furthermore, senior police officials highlighted a significant operational limitation to the *Hindustan Times*: while police can inspect designated markets, check commercial transport vehicles, and respond to public telephone complaints, conducting continuous foot patrols inside dense, labyrinthine residential neighbourhoods to identify individual households bursting crackers during prohibited hours is practically impossible. Enforcement in residential colonies must inevitably rely on post-facto response rather than continuous physical prevention.

## The Verification Chain: From NEERI Formulations to the Retail Box

The integrity of the green-cracker policy depends upon an unbroken verification chain linking three distinct institutional actors: scientific institutions, manufacturing enterprises, and regulatory enforcement agencies.

CSIR-NEERI operates the National Centre of Excellence for Green Firecrackers. On its official institutional portal, NEERI emphasizes that only registration certificates and test reports issued through its official online portal are authentic and legally valid. NEERI's regulatory mandate is product-specific: it evaluates chemical formulations submitted by manufacturers holding valid explosive licenses from PESO or District Magistrates, conducts laboratory emissions testing, and issues formal approval certificates.

However, the institutional hand-off between formulation approval and retail packaging verification has historically created regulatory ambiguity. In an official press note issued on 16 October 2025, CSIR-NEERI explicitly clarified the division of responsibilities: NEERI develops green cracker formulations, conducts laboratory emission tests, and issues registration letters to manufacturers; it does not print, issue, or physically affix barcodes or QR codes onto retail packages. Instead, manufacturers are responsible for developing product-specific QR codes, while regulatory authorities—including PESO, the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB), State Pollution Control Boards, and police—are tasked with verifying those codes in the market.

Under the Supreme Court's 2026 framework, this verification requirement has become substantially more demanding. The Court's order mandates not merely a product-level QR code, but a *batch-specific, non-transferable* QR code that binds a particular physical consignment to its specific manufacturing date, licensed facility, and laboratory test report. 

This requirement introduces an untested technical question: what digital resolver system exists for field verification? If a QR code simply encodes an alphanumeric string that directs a smartphone browser to a manufacturer's public website, it does not prevent an unscrupulous operator from copying a genuine QR code from an approved batch and printing thousands of duplicate stickers onto boxes containing banned barium fireworks. 

Authentic verification requires a secure, tamper-resistant system where scanning the code queries an authoritative central registry hosted by NEERI or PESO, displays the specific authorized batch number, and provides enforcement officers with a cryptographic means of confirming that the physical consignment in front of them is legitimate. Whether such a real-time digital verification system is fully operational across NCR retail markets ahead of Diwali remains an unanswered question on the public record.

## The 2025 Evidence Failure and Judicial Skepticism

The Supreme Court's rigorous oversight of the 2026 Diwali framework must be understood in the context of the regulatory failure that preceded it. The 2026 order explicitly recounts the history of the previous year's intervention. 

In October 2025, the Supreme Court granted a similar temporary relaxation permitting green crackers, intended in significant part to allow state authorities and environmental regulators to gather empirical data on whether green crackers could be managed effectively and how permitted use impacted ambient air quality. 

In paragraph 4 of the 29 October 2026 order, the Court recorded an unequivocal finding: authorities had failed to place satisfactory material before the Court demonstrating that the 2025 relaxation was effectively regulated or that comprehensive scientific monitoring was executed.

This judicial finding is crucial. It demonstrates that the 2026 experiment is not being conducted on a clean slate; it is being conducted against an official judicial record showing that the previous year's regulatory trial failed to generate the verifiable evidence the judiciary demanded. 

The Court also reiterated its deep concern that conventional, highly polluting firecrackers continued to be manufactured, trafficked, and sold under the guise of green crackers despite an ostensible total ban. This persistent regulatory leakage was precisely why the Court opted for a tightly regulated, time-bound legal window accompanied by heavy oversight obligations, rather than relying exclusively on an unmonitored blanket prohibition that had proven unenforceable in practice.

## Why "Green" Does Not Mean Non-Polluting

Public discourse frequently conflates the regulatory classification "green firecracker" with an environmental claim that the products are clean, harmless, or non-polluting. This is a scientific fallacy.

According to CSIR-NEERI's published technical criteria, green firecrackers are defined by comparative emission reductions achieved under controlled laboratory testing relative to conventional formulations in the same product category:
- A minimum particulate matter (PM) reduction of **30 percent**; or
- A minimum particulate matter reduction of **20 percent** combined with a minimum **10 percent reduction in gaseous emissions** (such as sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides).

These benchmarks represent significant chemical engineering achievements, primarily realized by eliminating potassium nitrate and sulfur, substituting barium compounds with alternative oxidizers, and incorporating chemical additives that act as dust suppressants. 

However, a 30 percent reduction against a highly toxic conventional baseline is not zero pollution. Green firecrackers are pyrotechnic devices that function through rapid exothermic oxidation. When detonated, they inevitably discharge fine particulate matter (PM2.5 and PM10), carbon monoxide, sulfur dioxide, nitrogen oxides, and aerosolized trace metals into the lower atmosphere. 

When tens of thousands of households burst green crackers simultaneously across a metropolitan area within a compressed two-hour window, the aggregate chemical loading on the ambient airshed remains massive. Classifying a product as "green" establishes that it meets a comparative manufacturing standard; it does not confer ambient environmental harmlessness.

## Air Quality Monitoring: Transparent Methodology vs. Simplistic Assumptions

To ensure that the 2026 experiment generates the empirical data that was missing in 2025, the Supreme Court directed the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB), in active coordination with the State Pollution Control Boards of Delhi, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, and Rajasthan, to publish daily Air Quality Index (AQI) reports across three mandatory temporal windows:
1. **Pre-Diwali Baseline Window:** 2 November to 6 November 2026.
2. **Permitted Diwali Window:** 7 November and 8 November 2026.
3. **Post-Diwali Observation Window:** 9 November to 30 November 2026.

In addition to continuous gaseous and particulate monitoring, the Court ordered specialized environmental sampling of soil, sand, and surface water across designated locations in Delhi-NCR to test for chemical residue and heavy metal deposition.

However, interpreting ambient air quality data requires scientific discipline. A common journalistic error is to observe an AQI of 320 on the morning of Diwali and an AQI of 450 the following morning, and attribute the entire numerical surge to firecrackers. Atmospheric chemistry in Delhi-NCR during early November is an extraordinarily complex, multi-variable system governed by seasonal meteorological transitions.

As documented in official technical explainers by the Delhi Government and CPCB:
- The composite city-wide AQI is an arithmetic average calculated from reporting Continuous Ambient Air Quality Monitoring Stations (CAAQMS).
- Monitoring stations that fail to transmit data or suffer technical dropouts in the preceding two hours are automatically excluded from the calculation, and real-time hourly numbers remain provisional pending manual validation by CPCB scientists.
- During early November, north-westerly surface winds frequently transport intense plumes of agricultural residue smoke from stubble burning in Punjab and Haryana into the Delhi bowl.
- Concurrently, post-monsoon seasonal cooling creates sharp atmospheric temperature inversions and collapses the planetary boundary layer mixing height from over 1,500 metres during the day to below 200 metres at night, trapping local emissions from vehicular traffic, road dust, and industrial units close to ground level regardless of fireworks.

Isolating the specific marginal contribution of green firecrackers requires far more than comparing two daily headline AQI numbers. A scientifically defensible analysis demands high-frequency station-level data completeness, chemical speciation of collected particulates (such as measuring specific tracer elements like barium, strontium, and potassium), and rigorous meteorological normalization modeling. Headline figures that ignore weather conditions and baseline emission sources produce advocacy, not evidence.

Furthermore, as of 3 November 2026, the permitted bursting window (7–8 November) and the post-Diwali observation period (9–30 November) have not occurred. Any definitive claim regarding the post-Diwali environmental impact of the 2026 relaxation is scientifically impossible at this juncture because the physical data does not yet exist.

## Public Health Monitoring: The Challenge of Clinical Attribution

A parallel accountability mandate in the Supreme Court's order requires health authorities to compile and submit aggregate hospital admission and emergency department attendance data for respiratory illnesses, burn trauma, and ocular injuries across Delhi-NCR through 30 November 2026.

Tracking healthcare utilization provides vital public health visibility. However, drawing direct causal inferences between firecracker emissions and aggregate hospital admissions requires extreme methodological caution.

Emergency room presentations for acute bronchial asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) exacerbations, and pediatric respiratory infections predictably surge across Northern India every autumn. This seasonal illness pattern is driven by cold weather transitions, circulating viral pathogens, and baseline regional smog. 

Determining whether an observed increase in hospital admissions during early November was caused specifically by green cracker smoke requires:
1. A consistent, pre-defined institutional coverage network across government and private tertiary hospitals;
2. Standardized clinical case definitions distinguishing blast/burn injuries from chronic respiratory complaints;
3. Comparison against a rigorous, multi-year epidemiological baseline; and
4. Statistical adjustment for ambient temperature drops and background PM2.5 exposure.

Health data covering the full monitoring period through 30 November is not yet available. Responsible journalism must report the existence of the judicial health-reporting mandate while making it explicit that empirical outcome data is pending and that causal conclusions cannot be drawn prematurely.

## What the Public Record Must Disclose Ahead of 14 December

The Supreme Court has directed all participating regulatory and enforcement agencies to submit their comprehensive compliance affidavits and monitoring reports by 14 December 2026, ahead of the next formal compliance hearing listed for 16 December.

Before that judicial review takes place, the public is entitled to examine the primary documentary evidence supporting each stage of the regulatory framework. A transparent accounting requires clear answers to fundamental administrative questions:
1. **Designated Sales Locations:** Where are the formal district notifications and public maps identifying the designated sales sites notified by District Magistrates and Police Commissioners?
2. **Trader Licensing:** Exactly how many temporary retail licenses were formally approved or restored in each NCR district for the 4–6 November window?
3. **Market Inspection & QR Auditing:** How many physical retail inspections were conducted by police and administrative teams? How many individual product QR codes were scanned, what digital validation tools were used, and what proportion resolved to authentic, batch-specific test certificates?
4. **Chemical Sampling & Testing:** How many product samples were confiscated from retail counters and dispatched to PESO laboratories for chemical analysis to verify the absence of banned barium salts?
5. **Enforcement & Forfeiture:** What are the reconciled, consolidated figures for firecracker seizures, FIRs registered, arrests made, and monetary security deposits forfeited across NCR?
6. **Air Quality Completeness:** Which CAAQMS monitoring stations maintained complete data uptime throughout the 7–8 November period, and what do chemical speciation and meteorological models indicate regarding the specific contribution of pyrotechnics?
7. **Hospital Surveillance:** What standardized reporting methodology was employed across healthcare facilities, and how do emergency admission rates compare against historical non-bursting periods?

These inquiries are not bureaucratic footnotes. They represent the precise evidentiary standard that separates an enforceable regulatory system from a symbolic paper exercise.

The ultimate test of Delhi-NCR's green-cracker experiment is not whether an attractive green label is pasted onto a cardboard box, nor whether a court issues a finely balanced order. The real test is whether public authorities can prove that the rules were enforced in the markets, that unauthorized products were intercepted, and that the environmental and public-health consequences are measured with uncompromising scientific rigor.

Until those compliance records, laboratory reports, and complete datasets are placed on the public record and subjected to independent scrutiny, neither effective regulatory enforcement nor the environmental safety of the experiment can be assumed.
