## Preamble

The Breakdown is not a website. It is an epistemic environment — a place where the architecture itself teaches, where navigation is explanation, where every interaction either deepens understanding or reveals its absence.

Volume III defines the constitutional rules governing every narrative surface across the platform. It replaces no existing system. It extends the Editorial Constitution's principles into the experience layer — the domain where canonical knowledge becomes reader understanding.

The central tension of this document is irreducible: narrative must be compelling enough to hold attention, yet honest enough to never manufacture certainty. The Breakdown resolves this tension by treating narrative as a projection of evidence, not a substitute for it. A story is a lens, not a conclusion. A chapter is an investigation, not a verdict.

Every recommendation in this document optimises for one outcome: **the reader understands something they did not understand before.** If a design choice improves engagement but not understanding, it violates this constitution. If a design choice sacrifices beauty for accuracy, it obeys it.

# I. Platform Narrative Philosophy

## 1.1 What the Platform Is

The Breakdown is a knowledge operating system disguised as a website. It appears to be a place where you read about policy, history, and evidence. It is, in fact, a place where you acquire the capacity to evaluate claims about the world.

The distinction matters. A website delivers content. A knowledge operating system develops capability. Every screen, every transition, every recommendation must be evaluated against this distinction: does this teach the reader to think better, or merely to consume more?

## 1.2 Reader Perception

The reader should perceive The Breakdown as a place where complexity is respected, not simplified away. Where disagreement is presented as evidence of intellectual honesty, not as a failure of editorial judgment. Where the platform says "we do not know" with the same confidence that other publications say "clearly" and "obviously."

The reader should feel that they are investigating alongside the editorial team, not being told what to think by it. The relationship is between investigator and evidence, with the platform serving as guide, advocate, and verifier — never as authority.

## 1.3 Distinction from Peers

The Breakdown is not Wikipedia. Wikipedia aspires to neutral encyclopaedic coverage. The Breakdown aspires to deep understanding of contested questions. Wikipedia tells you what happened. The Breakdown tells you why it matters, what the evidence shows, where historians disagree, and why you should care.

The Breakdown is not Reuters. Reuters reports events. The Breakdown contextualises them. A Reuters dispatch says "India's GDP grew 6.1%." The Breakdown asks "compared to what, measured how, and who disagrees?"

The Breakdown is not The Economist. The Economist offers analysis with institutional confidence. The Breakdown offers evidence with institutional transparency. The Economist's editorial voice is authoritative. The Breakdown's editorial voice is investigative — it shows you the evidence and invites you to reach your own conclusion.

The Breakdown is not Bloomberg. Bloomberg terminals optimise for speed and density of financial data. The Breakdown optimises for depth and context of public-policy knowledge. Bloomberg tells you what the market is doing. The Breakdown tells you what the evidence says about what should be done.

The Breakdown is not Our World in Data. Our World in Data presents empirical data beautifully. The Breakdown embeds that data in narrative, counterargument, and editorial judgment. Data without narrative is a dashboard. The Breakdown is not a dashboard.

The Breakdown is not a traditional newspaper. Newspapers report. The Breakdown explains. The temporal orientation is different: newspapers ask "what happened today?" The Breakdown asks "what do we know, how do we know it, and what should we do about it?"

The Breakdown is not an academic journal. Academic journals serve specialist audiences with disciplinary conventions. The Breakdown serves any intelligent reader with accessible prose and transparent evidence chains. The rigour is comparable. The accessibility is not.

The Breakdown is not an AI chatbot. Chatbots generate plausible text from training data. The Breakdown generates structured knowledge from verified evidence. A chatbot can produce a paragraph about MGNREGA in seconds. The Breakdown can tell you exactly which claims are supported, which are contested, and where the evidence gaps are. The difference is epistemological, not technological.

## 1.4 The Irreducible Principle

Narrative emerges from evidence. Evidence never emerges from narrative. This is the first principle and the last resort. If any design choice — any screen, any interaction, any animation, any recommendation — violates this principle, it must be redesigned or removed.

**⚠ Tension — Inevitability vs. Manipulation.** The narrative experience should feel inevitable — each evidence beat leading naturally to the next question. But inevitability is exactly what curiosity-gap-driven engagement design (autoplay, infinite scroll, algorithmic feeds) also produces — and this platform explicitly rejects optimising for anything but understanding. The resolution: inevitability must come from the *logic of the evidence* (this claim raises an unresolved question, which the next claim addresses), never from UI mechanics that make leaving feel effortful. If a reader cannot close the tab easily, that is a dark pattern regardless of how good the writing is.

**⚠ Tension — Transportation vs. Critical Resistance.** Narrative transportation research (Green & Brock) demonstrates that readers absorbed into a story process embedded claims with *less* critical resistance. For a news outlet, that is a feature. For an evidence-first institution, it is a risk that must be designed around. The practical implication: every narrative arc needs at least one deliberate *friction beat* — a moment that explicitly invites skepticism ("Here is the strongest argument against what you just read") — placed at or near peak transportation, not after it has worn off. The existing Counterarguments and "Historians ask..." sections already serve this function; the narrative layer must ensure they land at the emotionally right moment, not just the structurally correct one.

# II. Global Reader Journey

## 2.1 The Lifecycle

The reader journey is not a funnel. It is a deepening. Each stage represents not increased engagement but increased understanding. The platform's success is measured not by how many readers reach the next stage, but by whether readers at every stage understand something they did not understand before.

**Anonymous Visitor.** The reader arrives with a question, a curiosity, or a need. They encounter evidence immediately. The first screen they see must teach something. Not tease. Not hook. Teach. If the visitor leaves having understood one thing they did not understand before, the platform has succeeded — even if they never return.

**Curious Reader.** The reader returns because the platform answered a question honestly, including the parts of the answer that were uncertain. Trust is built not by providing all answers but by being transparent about which answers are incomplete. The curious reader begins to explore related questions, following evidence trails rather than editorial recommendations.

**Returning Learner.** The reader develops a pattern of return driven by intellectual curiosity rather than notification or habit. They begin to bookmark, annotate, and track their learning across topics. The platform recognises this transition and offers structured paths — not because the reader needs to be guided, but because the reader is ready to be systematic.

**Scholar.** The reader engages with primary sources, engages with historiography, and begins to understand the methodology behind claims. The scholar reads footnotes, follows source chains, and notices when evidence is contested. The platform provides tools for this depth: source views, evidence chains, confidence indicators, and version histories.

**Researcher.** The reader uses The Breakdown as a research tool — citing it, comparing its evidence with other sources, identifying gaps in its coverage. The platform treats researchers as collaborators, not consumers. Researcher feedback improves the knowledge base.

**Contributor.** The reader contributes corrections, evidence, source suggestions, or editorial challenges. The contributor relationship is governed by the Editorial Constitution's correction policy. Every contribution is evaluated on evidence quality, not contributor reputation.

**Expert.** The reader becomes a subject-matter expert who reviews, validates, or challenges the platform's editorial judgments. The expert relationship is governed by the Gold Standard Review process. Expert contributions are published transparently alongside the editorial record.

**Institutional Partner.** Universities, newsrooms, policy organisations, and government bodies adopt The Breakdown as a reference source. The institutional relationship is governed by the Public Standards document. Institutional feedback shapes the platform's editorial priorities without compromising editorial independence.

## 2.2 Understanding Evolution

Understanding evolves through four layers, corresponding to the Evidence Spine:

**What Happened.** The reader acquires factual knowledge — events, dates, actors, outcomes. This is the surface layer. Every screen must deliver it.

**What the Evidence Shows.** The reader acquires methodological knowledge — how claims are verified, what evidence supports which assertions, where confidence is high or low. This is the layer where trust is built.

**Where Historians Disagree.** The reader acquires interpretive knowledge — that reasonable people examining the same evidence reach different conclusions. This is the layer where intellectual maturity is developed.

**Why It Matters.** The reader acquires contextual knowledge — the connection between historical evidence and present-day questions. This is the layer where understanding becomes wisdom.

The platform must never skip layers. A story that tells you what happened without showing you the evidence is journalism. A story that shows you evidence without acknowledging disagreement is propaganda. A story that acknowledges disagreement without explaining why it matters is academic hedging. The Breakdown does all four.

## 2.5 Reader Psychology

Three bodies of research govern the narrative experience layer:

**Narrative Transportation** (Green & Brock). Readers transported into a story process embedded claims with less critical resistance. For an evidence-first platform, this is a design risk: every arc needs a deliberate friction beat — a moment that explicitly invites skepticism — placed at or near peak transportation, not after it has worn off.

**Flow** (Csikszentmihalyi). Flow requires a match between challenge and skill. This grounds the Explorer/Scholar/Researcher reading-mode toggle as a skill-selector: the narrative layer reads a reader's chosen mode as a contract about how much friction and depth they have opted into, not just a formatting preference.

**Curiosity as Information Gap** (Loewenstein). An information gap poses a real, specific, answerable question. A mystery box withholds the answer to create anxiety. Every hook in this narrative system must be the former — a chapter should never open with rhetorical vagueness ("What really happened in 1947?") when it can open with the specific, falsifiable question the chapter actually investigates ("Was Partition avoidable, or did the Cabinet Mission Plan's collapse make it inevitable?").

These research foundations operate alongside the cognitive-load and curiosity-gap principles already embedded in the platform's evidence architecture.

# III. Homepage Experience

## 3.1 Constitutional Role

The homepage is not a landing page. It is a declaration of intellectual intent. It tells the reader: this is a place where evidence is respected, complexity is embraced, and understanding is the only currency that matters.

The homepage must answer one question in five seconds: "What kind of institution is this?" Not "what kind of content does it have?" Not "what topics does it cover?" The answer must be epistemological, not topical.

## 3.2 What Appears

The homepage presents:

- The institutional identity — what The Breakdown is and why it exists.
- The founding publication — the single most important thing the reader can engage with.
- The evidence chain — a transparent view of how knowledge is produced here.
- The trust dashboard — a real-time view of the institution's editorial health.
- The learning path — structured entry points for readers at different stages.

## 3.3 What Never Appears

The homepage must never contain:

- Engagement bait — "trending," "most read," "you won't believe."
- Advertising — in any form, including native advertising, sponsored content, or affiliate links.
- Social proof — follower counts, share buttons, "X people are reading this."
- Time pressure — "breaking," "just in," "live." The Breakdown does not compete on speed.
- Personalisation that obscures editorial judgment — the homepage should be the same for every reader, because editorial priorities are not determined by reader behaviour.

## 3.4 Evolution

The homepage evolves as the knowledge base grows. It begins as a single-chapter showcase. It becomes a multi-volume library. It eventually becomes a knowledge dashboard where readers can see the institution's coverage breadth, depth, and freshness at a glance. The evolution is organic, driven by editorial completion, not by feature roadmaps.

# IV. Story World Experience

## 4.1 Identity

A Story World is a bounded investigation into a question that cannot be answered by a single story. "India and the World" is a Story World. "The Indian Constitution" is a Story World. Each has its own identity — its own question, its own evidence base, its own editorial voice.

A Story World is not a category. It is not a tag. It is not a folder. It is an intellectual territory with defined boundaries, internal structure, and relationships to other Story Worlds.

## 4.2 Purpose

The purpose of a Story World is to organise knowledge around questions, not around topics. "Agriculture" is a topic. "Why do Indian farmers remain poor despite decades of reform?" is a question. Story Worlds are built around questions.

## 4.3 Relationships

Story Worlds relate to each other through shared entities, shared claims, shared evidence, and shared thinkers. The Knowledge Graph makes these relationships visible. A reader investigating "India's 1962 war" in the India and the World Story World can follow entity links to "Nehru's worldview" — which connects to the Indian Constitution Story World's coverage of Article 19.

## 4.4 Growth

A Story World grows when new chapters, investigations, or evidence are added. Growth is not measured by word count or asset count. It is measured by the number of questions that can now be answered which could not be answered before.

## 4.5 Navigation

Story World navigation follows a question hierarchy: the Story World's central question decomposes into sub-questions, which decompose into individual investigations or stories. The reader enters at whatever level of granularity matches their interest and can always zoom out to see the larger question.

## 4.6 Visual Rhythm

Each Story World establishes a visual rhythm — a pattern of density, breathing space, and evidence presentation that becomes familiar over time. The rhythm is not imposed by a template. It emerges from the content's natural cadence: dense evidence sections alternate with reflective passages; quantitative data alternates with human stories.

## 4.7 Reader Progression

The Story World guides progression from overview to depth. A reader who enters at the surface level encounters the Story World's question, its central claim, and the evidence supporting it. A reader who drills deeper encounters historiography, counterarguments, primary sources, and data. The progression is always voluntary — the platform never locks content behind prerequisites.

# V. Story Experience

## 5.1 Constitutional Blueprint

Every story is an investigation. It has a beginning that states the question, a middle that presents the evidence, and an end that synthesises the findings — while remaining honest about what remains unknown.

## 5.2 Opening

The opening must accomplish three things in under 300 words: state the question, establish the stakes, and show the reader why this question has not been adequately answered before. The opening is never a summary. It is an invitation to investigate.

## 5.3 Question

Every story declares its central question explicitly. The question is not buried in the third paragraph. It is not implied by the headline. It is stated in the opening section, often in the hero area, and always in the structured metadata. The question is the story's constitutional identity.

## 5.4 Evidence

Evidence is presented in layers: the claim, the supporting evidence, the source, the confidence level, and the counter-evidence. No claim appears without at least one supporting source. No source appears without a confidence indicator. No confidence indicator appears without an explanation of how it was determined.

## 5.5 Conflict

Every story presents the central conflict — the tension between competing interpretations, competing evidence, or competing values. A story without conflict is a brochure, not journalism.

## 5.6 Counterargument

Every story includes the strongest counter-argument to its central claim. The counter-argument is presented fairly, not as a straw man. The reader must be able to evaluate the counter-argument on its merits before the story explains why the editorial team reached its conclusion.

## 5.7 Reflection

Every story includes a moment of reflection — a passage where the editorial team steps back from the evidence and asks what it means. This is where editorial judgment is exercised transparently, with explicit acknowledgment that the judgment is editorial, not factual.

## 5.8 Continuation

Every story ends with a continuation — a clear indication of what the reader should investigate next. This may be another story, a related investigation, a counter-argument to explore, or a learning path. The continuation is never a cliff-hanger. It is an intellectual breadcrumb.

## 5.9 Uncertainty

Every story explicitly acknowledges what it does not know. The uncertainty section is not an afterthought. It is a constitutional requirement. A story that does not acknowledge its limitations is incomplete by definition.

## 5.10 Knowledge Connections

Every story links to related knowledge objects — entities, claims, evidence, timelines, maps, and thinkers. These connections are not "related articles" in the journalistic sense. They are graph edges that reveal the structure of knowledge itself.

## 5.11 Emotional Arc Templates

A single universal emotional arc does not fit all content types. Forcing "Hope" and "Action" beats after a chapter on mass violence or historical trauma would be tone-deaf — exactly the kind of manufactured uplift a serious reader would flag immediately.

**Two arc templates, not one universal shape:**

**Investigation Arc** (for unresolved historical and social questions — Partition, Kashmir, the India-China border): Curiosity → Confusion → Discovery → Understanding → Reflection → Open Questions. It ends in honest uncertainty, which is the correct emotional resting point for content the historiography already tells us remains contested.

**Policy Arc** (for The Fix content — EV charging infrastructure, electoral finance reform, MGNREGA): Curiosity → Confusion → Discovery → Understanding → Reflection → Hope → Action. These chapters genuinely end in "here is what is being tried, here is what a reader can look for next" — the Hope and Action beats are earned by the content type, not imposed on it.

Every story declares which arc template it uses as a piece of metadata (a thin authoring choice, not a new canonical object) so the narrative layer never applies the wrong emotional shape to the wrong subject matter. The arc template does not alter the evidence chain — it governs the emotional sequencing of how evidence is revealed.

## 5.12 Learning Outcomes

Every story declares what the reader will understand after reading it. These outcomes are not marketing copy. They are editorial commitments. If a story claims the reader will understand "why Panchsheel failed," then the story must deliver that understanding or acknowledge that it could not.

# VI. Investigation Experience

## 6.1 Lifecycle

An investigation is a living document. It begins with a question, proceeds through evidence collection, reaches a preliminary conclusion, and then evolves as new evidence emerges. The lifecycle is: Question → Evidence → Preliminary Finding → Expert Review → Publication → Update → Revision → Archive.

## 6.2 Evidence Evolution

Investigations track evidence evolution explicitly. Version 1.0 may present a preliminary finding based on available evidence. Version 1.1 may update that finding in light of newly discovered documents. Version 2.0 may reverse a conclusion based on counter-evidence. Every version is preserved. Nothing is overwritten.

## 6.3 Updates

Updates are published with explicit changelogs: what changed, why it changed, what evidence prompted the change, and what the previous version stated. The reader can always see the full version history of any investigation.

## 6.4 Historical Revisions

When an investigation's conclusion changes, the previous conclusion is not deleted. It is archived with an explanation of why the revision occurred. This preserves the intellectual history of the investigation and prevents the platform from appearing to have always been right.

## 6.5 New Discoveries

The platform actively seeks new evidence that might challenge existing findings. This is not a passive process. The Verification Bureau maintains a research workflow that periodically re-examines published investigations against new sources.

## 6.6 Reader Participation

Readers can contribute evidence, challenge findings, and suggest corrections. Contributions are evaluated on evidence quality, not contributor identity. Every published correction is logged in the Book of Record with full provenance.

# VII. Knowledge Graph Experience

## 7.1 The Principle

The Knowledge Graph must explain. It must never overwhelm. Its purpose is to make the structure of knowledge visible — to show readers how claims relate to evidence, how evidence relates to sources, how sources relate to stories, and how stories relate to each other.

## 7.2 Entry

The reader enters the Knowledge Graph through any knowledge object — a story, a claim, an entity, a timeline, a map. The graph is always one click away, but never forced. The reader who does not want to explore the graph should never feel that the platform is incomplete without it.

## 7.3 Exploration

Graph exploration follows relationship edges. A story links to its claims, which link to their evidence, which link to their sources. An entity links to the stories that discuss it, the claims that reference it, and the evidence that supports or challenges those claims. Exploration is guided by curiosity, not by algorithm.

## 7.4 Relationships

Relationships are typed and labelled. "Source supports Claim" is a different relationship than "Source challenges Claim." "Story discusses Entity" is a different relationship than "Story depends on Evidence." The reader can always see the type and direction of a relationship.

## 7.5 Discovery

Discovery in the Knowledge Graph is serendipitous but structured. The reader who explores a story's claims may discover an entity they had not encountered, which leads to a different story, which leads to a new question. The graph rewards curiosity without creating confusion.

## 7.6 Context

Every node in the graph carries context: its type, its confidence level, its last verification date, and its relationships to other nodes. The reader never encounters an isolated piece of knowledge. Every fact is embedded in its evidential context.

## 7.7 Zooming

The reader can zoom in and out of the graph. Zooming in reveals detail: individual claims, individual sources, individual evidence items. Zooming out reveals structure: how stories relate, how evidence clusters, how questions connect. The zoom level is controlled by the reader, not by the platform.

## 7.8 Abstraction

At the highest level of abstraction, the Knowledge Graph reveals the platform's intellectual architecture: which questions have been investigated, which remain open, where evidence is strong, and where it is weak. This view serves researchers, editors, and institutional partners who need to assess the platform's coverage.

# VIII. Search Experience

## 8.1 The Principle

Search is inquiry, not retrieval. When a reader types a query, they are not asking the platform to find a document. They are asking the platform to help them understand something. The search experience must honour this distinction.

## 8.2 Intent

Search must distinguish between four types of intent: factual ("what is MGNREGA"), analytical ("why did Panchsheel fail"), comparative ("how does India's judicial system compare to others"), and navigational ("show me the story about Kashmir"). Each intent type produces a different result format.

## 8.3 Answers

Search results should include direct answers where possible. If the reader asks "what is the evidence grade of the MGNREGA Fix," the search should return the answer, not a list of documents that might contain the answer.

## 8.4 Exploration

Search should suggest related questions. A reader who searches for "Kashmir 1948" should be presented with related entities, related claims, and related investigations — not just related keywords.

## 8.5 Learning

Search should identify learning opportunities. A reader who searches for "non-alignment" should be shown not just the definition, but the investigation that explains why it mattered, the counter-arguments that challenged it, and the evidence that supports or undermines it.

## 8.6 Confidence

Search results must carry confidence indicators. A reader searching for a fact should know whether the platform considers the answer well-established, contested, or uncertain.

## 8.7 Ambiguity

Search must handle ambiguity honestly. If the reader's query could refer to multiple topics, the search should present the possibilities and ask the reader to clarify — not silently guess the most popular interpretation.

## 8.8 Unknowns

Search must acknowledge what it does not know. If the reader's query falls outside the platform's coverage, the search should say so explicitly, rather than returning irrelevant results or empty pages.

# IX. AI Experience

## 9.1 Constitutional AI

AI in The Breakdown is a tool for understanding, not a source of knowledge. Every AI interaction must be bounded by canonical claims, transparent about its sources, and explicit about its limitations.

## 9.2 Permitted Functions

AI may:

- **Explain.** Translate complex evidence into accessible language without distorting its meaning.
- **Teach.** Guide readers through learning paths, adapting to their level without lowering the evidence standard.
- **Compare.** Structure comparisons between claims, policies, or evidence using canonical data.
- **Challenge.** Present counter-arguments and alternative interpretations drawn from the evidence base.
- **Translate.** Make content accessible across language barriers without altering its substance.
- **Connect.** Identify relationships between knowledge objects that the reader might not discover independently.
- **Summarise.** Compress complex investigations into structured summaries that preserve the evidence chain.

## 9.3 Prohibited Functions

AI must never:

- **Hallucinate.** Generate claims that are not supported by canonical evidence.
- **Speculate.** Offer opinions or predictions that go beyond what the evidence supports.
- **Invent.** Create sources, quotes, or data that do not exist in the canonical layer.
- **Hide uncertainty.** Present uncertain claims with the same confidence as established facts.
- **Replace editors.** Make editorial judgments about what to publish, what to retract, or how to frame a question.
- **Replace evidence.** Substitute generated text for cited, verified evidence.

## 9.4 Bounded Interactions

Every AI interaction must display its source layer: which canonical claims it drew from, what confidence levels those claims carry, and what the evidence gaps are. The reader must always be able to trace the AI's output back to its evidential foundation.

## 9.5 Transparency

AI interactions are logged in the Book of Record. Every AI-generated summary, explanation, or comparison is versioned, attributed, and reviewable. AI output is never presented as editorial judgment without explicit labelling.

## 9.6 The Red Line

If an AI interaction cannot be bounded by canonical evidence — if the reader's question falls outside the platform's knowledge base — the AI must say so. "I can explain what the evidence shows about X. I cannot tell you what the evidence shows about Y because we have not investigated Y." This is not a limitation. It is a feature.

# X. Reader Memory

## 10.1 The Principle

The platform remembers readers to serve their learning, not to serve the platform's engagement metrics. Memory is a tool for understanding, not a mechanism for retention.

## 10.2 What the Platform Remembers

**Reading History.** The platform records which stories, investigations, and evidence the reader has engaged with. This enables the platform to suggest next steps and to avoid repeating content the reader has already encountered.

**Learning Frontier.** The platform tracks the reader's demonstrated knowledge level across topics and suggests the next logical step in their learning journey. The frontier is computed from reading patterns, not from self-reported preferences.

**Bookmarks.** The reader can save knowledge objects for later engagement. Bookmarks are private and persistent. They are never shared, sold, or used for advertising.

**Investigations.** The reader can track their own research progress through investigations they are following. This enables the reader to see how investigations evolve over time.

**Collections.** The reader can organise knowledge objects into personal collections for research, teaching, or study purposes. Collections are private by default and shareable by explicit action.

**Reading Streaks.** The platform tracks consecutive days of reading, not as a gamification mechanism, but as a learning rhythm indicator. Streaks are visible only to the reader.

**Progress.** The platform tracks the reader's progress through learning paths and structured content. Progress is measured by knowledge acquisition, not by time spent.

## 10.3 Where Memory Stops

Memory must stop at the boundary of surveillance. The platform must never:

- Track reading behaviour for advertising purposes.
- Share reading data with third parties without explicit consent.
- Use reading patterns to manipulate the reader's behaviour.
- Create profiles that predict the reader's political views, purchasing decisions, or personal characteristics.
- Retain data longer than necessary for the stated learning purpose.

## 10.4 The Surveillance Boundary

The distinction between service and surveillance is intent. Remembering that a reader completed a learning path serves the reader. Remembering that a reader spent 47 seconds on a page about Kashmir serves the platform. The Breakdown remembers to serve the reader. Full stop.

# XI. Narrative Navigation

## 11.1 The Principle

Navigation is explanation. Every navigation path should teach the reader something about the structure of knowledge. The reader should always understand where they are, how they got there, and what their options are.

## 11.2 Enter

The reader enters knowledge through any surface: a story, a search result, a recommendation, a direct link. Every entry point must orient the reader immediately: what is this? why does it matter? where should I go next?

## 11.3 Exit

Every exit from a knowledge object must offer the reader a meaningful continuation. The reader who finishes a story should never face a dead end. The continuation is editorially curated — not algorithmically generated — and reflects the institution's judgment about what the reader should investigate next.

## 11.4 Resume

The platform enables readers to resume interrupted investigations. If a reader leaves a story halfway through, the platform remembers where they stopped and offers to continue from that point. Resumption is a convenience, not a compulsion.

## 11.5 Branch

Readers can branch from any knowledge object to related objects. Branching is always available but never forced. The branching interface must make clear what the reader is leaving and what they are entering.

## 11.6 Explore

Exploration is the platform's most important navigation mode. The reader can move freely between stories, investigations, entities, claims, and evidence. Exploration is guided by the Knowledge Graph's relationship edges, not by algorithmic recommendations.

## 11.7 Return

The platform enables readers to return to previous positions in their reading journey. The return mechanism must be simple, fast, and non-disruptive. The reader should never feel lost.

## 11.8 Jump

Readers can jump directly to specific sections, claims, or evidence items via deep links, search, or table of contents. Jumps are instantaneous and preserve the reader's position in their navigation history.

## 11.9 Zoom

Readers can zoom between abstraction levels — from a high-level overview of a Story World down to a specific piece of evidence. Zooming is always reversible and always preserves context.

# XII. Learning Experience

## 12.1 The Principle

The platform is a learning system. Every interaction should either teach something new or reinforce something already learned. Learning is not a feature. It is the platform's reason for existing.

## 12.2 Learning Paths

Learning paths are structured sequences of knowledge objects designed to build understanding progressively. Paths are editorially curated, not algorithmically generated. They follow the principle of progressive complexity: each step assumes the reader has mastered the previous one.

## 12.3 Mastery

Mastery is measured by the reader's ability to apply knowledge, not by their ability to recall facts. The platform assesses mastery through reflection prompts, evidence evaluation exercises, and structured comparisons — not through quizzes or multiple-choice tests.

## 12.4 Reflection

Every learning path includes reflection points — moments where the reader is asked to synthesise what they have learned, identify remaining questions, and consider alternative interpretations. Reflection is not optional. It is a constitutional requirement for learning paths.

## 12.5 Revision

The platform supports spaced repetition of key concepts, evidence, and claims. Revision is triggered by the learning frontier, not by notification. The reader is never pressured to revise. They are invited.

## 12.6 Knowledge Reinforcement

Key knowledge is reinforced through multiple exposures across different contexts. A claim encountered in a story is reinforced when the reader encounters the same claim in a different story, a timeline, or a comparison. The platform makes these connections visible.

## 12.7 Retrieval Practice

The platform supports retrieval practice by occasionally asking the reader to recall information they have previously encountered. This is done respectfully, with clear indication that the purpose is learning reinforcement, not assessment.

## 12.8 Progressive Complexity

Learning paths begin with overview and progress to detail. They begin with established facts and progress to contested interpretations. They begin with single-discipline analysis and progress to multi-disciplinary synthesis. The progression is always voluntary.

# XIII. Annotation Experience

## 13.1 The Principle

Readers interact with knowledge. Annotation is how that interaction becomes explicit, portable, and useful. The annotation system must be as rigorous as the evidence system.

## 13.2 Notes

Readers can attach personal notes to any knowledge object. Notes are private by default. They are stored locally and synchronised only with explicit reader action. Notes are never shared, sold, or used for any purpose other than the reader's own learning.

## 13.3 Highlights

Readers can highlight passages within stories, investigations, and evidence. Highlights are persistent and searchable. They are never used to infer the reader's interests for advertising purposes.

## 13.4 Citations

Readers can generate citations for any knowledge object in standard academic formats. Citations include the version number, the last verification date, and the evidence grade. This enables readers to cite The Breakdown responsibly.

## 13.5 Questions

Readers can attach questions to specific passages. Questions are private by default and can be made public through explicit action. Public questions are reviewed by the Verification Bureau and may result in editorial updates.

## 13.6 Private Annotations

Private annotations are the reader's intellectual workspace. They are encrypted, persistent, and never accessible to the platform without explicit reader action.

## 13.7 Shared Annotations

Shared annotations enable collaborative research. When a reader shares an annotation, it becomes visible to other readers who have access to the same knowledge object. Shared annotations are attributed and versioned.

## 13.8 Research Workflows

The annotation system supports structured research workflows: collecting evidence, organising sources, tracking claims, and building arguments. These workflows are designed for researchers, journalists, and students who use The Breakdown as a research tool.

# XIV. Evidence Experience

## 14.1 The Principle

Evidence is the platform's foundation. The evidence experience must be as rich, as transparent, and as accessible as the narrative experience.

## 14.2 Primary Sources

Primary sources are presented with full provenance: archive, collection, shelfmark, date, author, and digital surrogate where available. The reader can always verify that a source exists and where it is held.

## 14.3 Documents

Documents are presented with transcription, translation (where applicable), and annotation. The reader can compare the original document with the editorial interpretation. Discrepancies are noted explicitly.

## 14.4 Citations

Citations follow a standard format and include the confidence level of the claim they support. The reader can trace any claim to its supporting citations and evaluate the citation's authority.

## 14.5 Confidence

Every piece of evidence carries a confidence indicator: High, Moderate, Low, or Contested. The indicator is computed from the evidence grade, the source authority, and the corroboration level. The computation is transparent and reproducible.

## 14.6 Provenance

Provenance chains are fully visible. The reader can trace a claim from its presentation in a story through the Claim Registry to the Evidence Registry to the Source Registry to the physical archive. No step in the chain is hidden.

## 14.7 Verification

Verification status is displayed for every claim and every source. The reader can see when a claim was last verified, by whom, and against what evidence. Verification is an ongoing process, not a one-time event.

## 14.8 Cross References

Cross references connect evidence across stories, investigations, and timelines. A piece of evidence cited in one story is cross-referenced to every other story that cites the same evidence. This creates a web of evidential relationships that reveals the platform's intellectual structure.

# XV. Comparison Experience

## 15.1 The Principle

Comparison maximises understanding. It never produces rankings for entertainment. A comparison that declares a "winner" without explaining why is a leaderboard, not journalism.

## 15.2 Comparison Subjects

Comparisons may involve countries, policies, leaders, economies, historical events, ideas, or claims. The comparison surface adapts to the subject matter while preserving the same structural principles: evidence first, transparency always, reader agency always.

## 15.3 Structural Principles

Every comparison must:

- Present the evidence for each subject independently before comparing.
- Identify the dimensions of comparison explicitly.
- Disclose evidence gaps for each subject.
- Avoid false precision — if the evidence does not support a comparison on a particular dimension, say so.
- Present editorial judgment as editorial judgment, not as objective ranking.

## 15.4 The Ranking Prohibition

Comparisons may identify which subject performs best on a specific, measurable dimension — but only when the evidence supports that identification. Comparisons must never produce overall rankings, scores, or leaderboards. The reader must be left to reach their own conclusion.

---

# XVI. Data Experience

## 16.1 The Principle

Quantitative evidence becomes narrative when it is contextualised. A number without context is a decoration. A number with context is knowledge.

## 16.2 Dashboards

Dashboards present quantitative evidence in structured layouts. Every dashboard must answer: what does this data show? how was it collected? what are its limitations? what should the reader conclude?

## 16.3 Charts

Charts must be legible to non-specialist readers. Every chart includes a title that states the conclusion, axis labels that explain the units, a source citation, and a confidence indicator. Charts never use colour alone to convey meaning.

## 16.4 Maps

Maps must be created from verified geospatial data. Disputed boundaries are shown with dashed lines and explanatory notes. Maps never present disputed borders as settled fact.

## 16.5 Timelines

Timelines present events in chronological order with evidence links. Every timeline event connects to the claim it supports and the source that documents it. Timelines are navigable and searchable.

## 16.6 Statistics

Statistics are presented with their confidence intervals, sample sizes, and methodology. The platform never presents a single number without its uncertainty range.

## 16.7 The Narrative Bridge

Every quantitative element must have a narrative bridge — a passage that explains what the number means, why it matters, and how it relates to the story's central question. The bridge is not optional.

---

# XVII. Recommendation Philosophy

## 17.1 The Principle

Recommendations emerge from evidence, not from engagement. The platform recommends what the reader should investigate next based on intellectual relationships, not popularity.

## 17.2 Recommendation Sources

Recommendations are derived from:

- **Unanswered questions** — the platform identifies questions that the reader's current engagement has raised but not answered.
- **Shared evidence** — the platform identifies knowledge objects that share evidence with the reader's current engagement.
- **Shared claims** — the platform identifies knowledge objects that reference the same claims.
- **Shared entities** — the platform identifies knowledge objects that discuss the same entities.
- **Reader intent** — the platform infers the reader's intellectual trajectory from their reading patterns.

## 17.3 What Recommendations Must Never Use

Recommendations must never use:

- **Popularity** — how many other readers engaged with a piece is irrelevant to its intellectual value.
- **Engagement metrics** — time on page, scroll depth, and click-through rates do not indicate understanding.
- **Advertising** — no recommendation may be influenced by commercial considerations.
- **Social signals** — likes, shares, and comments do not indicate quality.

## 17.4 Transparency

Every recommendation must be explainable. The reader must be able to ask "why are you showing me this?" and receive an answer grounded in evidence relationships. "Because other readers liked it" is never an acceptable answer.

# XVIII. Community Experience

## 18.1 The Principle

Community is how the platform maintains its integrity. Readers, experts, and institutions contribute to the platform's knowledge base through a structured, transparent, and evidence-governed process.

## 18.2 Corrections

Readers can submit corrections to any published claim. Corrections are evaluated on evidence quality, not contributor identity. Every published correction is logged in the Book of Record with full provenance. The correction process is governed by the Editorial Constitution's Corrections Policy.

## 18.3 Discussions

Discussions are structured around evidence, not opinion. Every discussion thread is tied to a specific claim, piece of evidence, or editorial judgment. Off-topic contributions are moderated. Personal attacks are prohibited. The discussion environment is designed for intellectual engagement, not social interaction.

## 18.4 Expert Review

Subject-matter experts can review published content and provide structured feedback. Expert review is credited and published alongside the content. The platform values disagreement from experts more than agreement.

## 18.5 Academic Review

Academic institutions can adopt The Breakdown as a teaching resource and provide feedback on its editorial accuracy, pedagogical effectiveness, and accessibility. Academic review is published transparently.

## 18.6 Public Feedback

Any reader can provide feedback on any aspect of the platform. Feedback is reviewed by the editorial team and published where it improves the platform's transparency. Critical feedback is valued more highly than praise.

## 18.7 Institutional Collaboration

Universities, newsrooms, policy organisations, and government bodies can collaborate with The Breakdown on research, verification, and publication. Institutional collaborations are governed by the Public Standards document and are published transparently.

# XIX. Accessibility Constitution

## 19.1 The Principle

Accessibility is not a feature. It is a constitutional requirement. Every experience on the platform must work for every reader, regardless of ability, device, or environment.

## 19.2 Screen Readers

Every screen must be fully navigable by screen reader. Every interactive element must have a descriptive label. Every image must have alt text. Every data visualisation must have a text alternative.

## 19.3 Keyboard Navigation

Every interaction must be completable using keyboard alone. Focus order must be logical and predictable. Focus indicators must be visible and high-contrast.

## 19.4 Switch Devices

Every interaction must be compatible with switch access devices. Timing must be adjustable. Complex interactions must have simplified alternatives.

## 19.5 Reduced Motion

Animations must respect the `prefers-reduced-motion` media query. Essential animations must have non-animated alternatives. Motion must never be the sole means of conveying information.

## 19.6 High Contrast

All text must meet WCAG AAA contrast ratios. Interactive elements must have sufficient contrast in all states. Colour must never be the sole means of conveying information.

## 19.7 Low Bandwidth

The platform must function on low-bandwidth connections. Critical content must be available without JavaScript. Images must be lazy-loaded and served in appropriate formats. Heavy assets must have lightweight alternatives.

## 19.8 Slow Devices

The platform must function on low-powered devices. Complex layouts must degrade gracefully. Animations must be disabled on devices that cannot render them smoothly.

## 19.9 Offline Reading

Readers must be able to save content for offline reading. Offline content must include the evidence chain, source citations, and knowledge graph links. Offline reading is a constitutional requirement, not a premium feature.

# XX. Mobile Narrative

## 20.1 The Principle

Mobile narrative adapts without becoming shallow. The evidence chain is preserved. The counter-arguments are preserved. The uncertainty is preserved. The screen is smaller. The integrity is not.

## 20.2 Adaptation Rules

On mobile devices:

- The evidence chain collapses into expandable sections, but is never removed.
- Counter-arguments appear after the main argument, not in a separate tab.
- Primary sources are accessible via the same link structure as desktop.
- Navigation is simplified but not eliminated.
- Data visualisations are replaced with accessible alternatives when they cannot render legibly.

## 20.3 What Mobile Must Not Do

Mobile must not:

- Remove evidence to save space.
- Simplify arguments to save reading time.
- Hide the confidence model.
- Remove the knowledge graph.
- Make the correction process inaccessible.

# XXI. Motion Philosophy

## 21.1 The Principle

Every animation must teach. Motion that does not teach is decoration. Decoration that does not teach violates the platform's constitutional commitment to evidence and understanding.

## 21.2 When Motion Exists

Motion is permitted when it:

- Reveals structure (e.g., a timeline expanding to show events in sequence).
- Shows change over time (e.g., a chart animating to reveal a trend).
- Guides attention to evidence (e.g., a claim highlighting its supporting source).
- Confirms reader action (e.g., a bookmark confirming a save).
- Indicates loading state (e.g., a progress indicator during data fetch).

## 21.3 When Motion Is Forbidden

Motion is forbidden when it:

- Is decorative (e.g., a parallax scroll effect that adds no information).
- Creates urgency (e.g., a countdown timer, a pulsing notification).
- Distracts from evidence (e.g., a hero animation that overshadows the content).
- Triggers vestibular disorders (e.g., rapid zooming, spinning, or shaking).
- Cannot be paused or reduced (e.g., autoplaying video backgrounds).

# XXII. Narrative Grammar

## 22.1 The Principle

Narrative has a grammar — a set of reusable primitives that combine to create complex investigations. This grammar is not a template. It is a vocabulary.

## 22.2 The Primitives

**Question.** A question the investigation seeks to answer. Questions are stated explicitly, never implied.

**Evidence.** A piece of evidence that supports, challenges, or complicates a claim. Evidence is always attributed to a source and carries a confidence indicator.

**Discovery.** A moment where the investigation reveals something unexpected. Discoveries are signposted explicitly and connected to the evidence that produced them.

**Transition.** A passage that moves the reader from one aspect of the investigation to another. Transitions explain why the reader is being moved and what they should carry with them.

**Counter-argument.** The strongest challenge to the investigation's central claim. Counter-arguments are presented fairly and evaluated on their merits.

**Reflection.** A passage where the editorial team steps back from the evidence and offers interpretive judgment. Reflections are labelled as editorial, not factual.

**Resolution.** A synthesis of the evidence that answers the investigation's central question — or acknowledges that the question cannot be fully answered.

**Open Question.** A question that the investigation could not answer, either because evidence is insufficient or because the question exceeds the investigation's scope. Open questions are published, not hidden.

**Synthesis.** A passage that connects the investigation's findings to broader questions, other investigations, or the reader's own understanding.

**Continuation.** A pointer to the next logical investigation, story, or learning step. Continuations are editorially curated, not algorithmically generated.

## 22.3 Combination Rules

These primitives combine according to rules:

- Every investigation must contain at least one Question, three Evidence items, one Counter-argument, and one Continuation.
- Every story must contain at least one Reflection and one Open Question.
- Every comparison must contain at least one Transition between subjects.
- Every Discovery must be followed by Evidence explaining the discovery.

# XXIII. Narrative Rhythm

## 23.1 The Principle

Narrative rhythm governs the pacing of knowledge delivery. Too fast, and the reader cannot absorb. Too slow, and the reader disengages. The rhythm must match the content's natural cadence.

## 23.2 Silence

Silence is the space between evidence. It is the moment where the reader is allowed to absorb what they have just learned before being presented with more. Silence is achieved through white space, short paragraphs, and visual breathing room. Silence is not emptiness. It is cognitive rest.

## 23.3 Density

Density is the concentration of evidence per unit of reading. High-density passages present multiple evidence items, source citations, and confidence indicators in rapid succession. Low-density passages present one claim and its supporting evidence with ample space for reflection.

## 23.4 Cognitive Load

The platform monitors cognitive load implicitly through narrative structure. After a high-density passage (e.g., a statistical analysis with multiple charts), the narrative must include a low-density passage (e.g., a reflection or a human story). The rhythm alternates.

## 23.5 Reading Cadence

Long-form investigations follow a specific cadence: introduction (low density) → context (medium density) → evidence (high density) → reflection (low density) → counter-argument (medium density) → synthesis (medium density) → continuation (low density). Short-form stories follow the same cadence in compressed form.

## 23.6 Long-Form

Long-form content (investigations, deep dives) must be broken into clearly labelled sections with navigation anchors. Each section should be completable in 5–10 minutes of reading. Sections should make sense independently while building on previous sections.

## 23.7 Short-Form

Short-form content (quick takes, evidence summaries) must be completable in 2–3 minutes. Short-form content must still include at least one evidence citation and one confidence indicator. Short-form does not mean shallow.

# XXIV. Anti-Patterns

## 24.1 The Principle

Anti-patterns are design choices that violate the platform's constitutional commitments. They are not "things we haven't built yet." They are things we must never build.

## 24.2 The Blacklist

**Engagement Bait.** "You won't believe what happened next." "This changes everything." "The truth about X." These phrases manufacture urgency where the evidence does not justify it. They violate the principle that narrative emerges from evidence.

**Dark Patterns.** Confirmation dialogs that guilt-trip the reader. Exit-intent popups. Autoplaying content. Hidden unsubscribe buttons. These patterns manipulate rather than inform. They violate the principle that readers remain investigators.

**Popularity Signals.** "Trending," "most read," "X people are reading this." These signals substitute social proof for evidence. They violate the principle that recommendations emerge from evidence, not engagement.

**False Urgency.** "Breaking," "just in," "live." The Breakdown does not compete on speed. It competes on understanding. False urgency creates anxiety where the evidence demands patience.

**Manufactured Certainty.** "Clearly," "obviously," "undoubtedly," "everyone knows." These phrases suppress legitimate disagreement. They violate the principle that uncertainty must be acknowledged.

**Attention Metrics as Product Metrics.** Time on page, scroll depth, click-through rates. These metrics measure attention, not understanding. They violate the principle that understanding is the optimisation target.

**Algorithmic Manipulation.** Using reading patterns to determine what the reader sees next, without editorial oversight. This violates the principle that recommendations emerge from evidence relationships.

**Personalisation That Obscures.** Showing different editorial content to different readers based on inferred preferences. Editorial judgment must be consistent. Personalisation may adapt presentation, never content.

**Fake Interactivity.** Clickbait slideshows, paginated articles, interactive quizzes that test recall rather than understanding. These patterns manufacture engagement where understanding would suffice.

**Emotional Manipulation.** Using images, music, or design to evoke emotions that the evidence does not justify. The platform respects the reader's emotional autonomy.

# XXV. Certification Framework

## 25.1 The Principle

Every experience must pass certification before publication. Certification is not a one-time gate. It is a standing requirement that applies to every screen, every interaction, and every transition.

## 25.2 Certification Areas

### Evidence First

**Requirement:** Every claim on the screen is supported by at least one cited source with a confidence indicator.

**Pass:** All claims have supporting sources. Confidence indicators are displayed. Source links are functional.

**Fail:** Any claim appears without supporting source. Confidence indicators are missing or inconsistent.

### Reader Agency

**Requirement:** The reader can navigate freely, exit at any point, and return to their previous position.

**Pass:** All navigation paths are reversible. No dead ends exist. The reader can always go back.

**Fail:** Any navigation path is irreversible. Any dead end exists. The reader is trapped.

### One Question Rule

**Requirement:** Every story or investigation declares its central question explicitly.

**Pass:** The question is stated in the opening, in the metadata, and in the structured data.

**Fail:** The question is implied but not stated. The question is buried in the text. The question is absent.

### Transparency

**Requirement:** Every editorial judgment is labelled as editorial. Every factual claim is labelled as factual. The distinction is never blurred.

**Pass:** Editorial sections are clearly distinguished from evidence sections. The reader can always tell what is fact and what is interpretation.

**Fail:** Editorial judgment is presented as fact. Factual claims are presented as opinion. The distinction is ambiguous.

### Accessibility

**Requirement:** The screen meets WCAG 2.2 AAA standards.

**Pass:** All text meets contrast ratios. All images have alt text. All interactions are keyboard-accessible. Screen reader navigation is complete.

**Fail:** Any accessibility standard is violated.

### Narrative Continuity

**Requirement:** The screen connects to at least one previous and one next knowledge object.

**Pass:** Continuation links are present. Related content is suggested. The reader is never left without a next step.

**Fail:** The screen is isolated. No connections are offered. The reader must search independently for what to do next.

### No Dead Ends

**Requirement:** Every screen offers at least one meaningful exit path that leads to deeper understanding.

**Pass:** Every screen has continuation links, related content suggestions, or a return path.

**Fail:** Any screen ends without a continuation.

### Cognitive Load

**Requirement:** The screen respects the reader's cognitive capacity. High-density sections are followed by low-density sections. Complex arguments are broken into digestible pieces.

**Pass:** The reading rhythm alternates between density and rest. Complex sections are clearly labelled and navigable.

**Fail:** The screen presents unrelieved density. Complex sections are not broken down. The reader is overwhelmed.

### Editorial Integrity

**Requirement:** The screen's editorial judgments are defensible under the Gold Standard Review criteria.

**Pass:** Every editorial judgment can be traced to evidence. Counter-arguments are presented fairly. Uncertainty is acknowledged.

**Fail:** Any editorial judgment cannot be defended against the Gold Standard Review criteria.

### Knowledge Gain

**Requirement:** The reader finishes the screen understanding something they did not understand before.

**Pass:** The screen teaches at least one new fact, provides at least one new perspective, or connects at least two previously unconnected pieces of knowledge.

**Fail:** The screen presents information the reader could have found elsewhere, without additional context, analysis, or connection.

### AI Safety

**Requirement:** Any AI interaction on the screen is bounded by canonical evidence, transparent about its sources, and explicit about its limitations.

**Pass:** AI output includes source citations. AI limitations are stated. AI output is distinguishable from editorial content.

**Fail:** AI output appears without sources. AI limitations are hidden. AI output is indistinguishable from editorial content.

# XXVI. Future Evolution

## 26.1 The Principle

This constitution must remain valid as the platform grows from thousands of claims to millions, from dozens of stories to hundreds of thousands, and from a single language to many. The principles are scale-invariant. The implementations must adapt.

## 26.2 Scale

At 10 million claims, the Knowledge Graph must remain traversable. The search experience must remain responsive. The comparison experience must remain useful. These are architectural requirements that this constitution imposes on future engineering work — without specifying how they are achieved.

## 26.3 Coverage

At hundreds of thousands of stories, the recommendation experience must remain evidence-based, not popularity-based. The learning experience must remain structured, not algorithmically generated. The homepage must remain a declaration of intellectual intent, not a content feed.

## 26.4 Language

When the platform operates in multiple languages, the evidence experience must remain consistent across translations. Translation must never alter the confidence model, the source citation system, or the editorial judgment. Translation is a projection, not a transformation.

## 26.5 Interfaces

When the platform supports voice interfaces, the narrative grammar must adapt to spoken delivery without losing its structural principles. When the platform supports spatial computing, the Knowledge Graph must become explorable in three dimensions without losing its legibility. When the platform supports AI tutors, the learning experience must remain bounded by canonical evidence without losing its personalisation.

## 26.6 Institutions

When universities adopt The Breakdown as a teaching resource, the certification framework must scale to institutional assessment. When newsrooms cite The Breakdown, the evidence experience must support journalistic verification standards. When government researchers use The Breakdown, the data experience must support policy analysis requirements.

## 26.7 The Constant

Through all of these changes, one principle remains: narrative emerges from evidence. Evidence never emerges from narrative. This is the constitutional constant. Everything else adapts.

## 26.8 Understanding Metrics

The platform measures understanding, not engagement. None of the following are engagement metrics — they are proxies for whether the reader actually learned something:

**Reflection-completion rate.** The proportion of chapter reads that reach the Reflection beat. This is a proxy for genuine comprehension rather than scroll depth.

**Counterargument engagement rate.** Whether readers who reach a Counterargument section actually expand or read it. This is a direct signal of whether the narrative friction beat is functioning.

**Cross-Journey return rate.** Readers who come back specifically for a related Journey via the graph-driven recommendation. This signals genuine extended curiosity rather than generic stickiness.

**Correction and feedback submission rate.** A healthy, rising rate is a trust signal, not a quality failure — exactly as the Methodology page argues.

**Reading-mode distribution.** The split between Explorer, Scholar, and Researcher over time. A maturing, trusted platform should see this distribution shift toward deeper modes as its audience becomes more engaged with the material, not just the interface.

**Self-reported understanding delta.** A single optional micro-question at the Reflection beat: "Do you understand this topic better than when you started?" This is the most direct available proxy for the platform's actual stated goal.

These metrics appear alongside technical events in analytics dashboards. They define product success, not engagement. See also `AGENTS.md` §Understanding Metrics for the engineering implementation of these measurements.

This document was reviewed by the Narrative Architecture Council on 28 July 2026. It was ratified as the constitutional governance document for all narrative experiences across The Breakdown platform.

Every future narrative decision must be traceable to a principle in this document. If a design choice cannot be justified by this document, either the document is incomplete or the design choice is out of scope.

The deepest tension in this document is irreducible: narrative must be compelling enough to hold attention, yet honest enough to never manufacture certainty. The Breakdown resolves this by treating narrative as a *structuring device*, never as a *persuasion technique*.
