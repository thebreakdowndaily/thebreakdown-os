# Competitor Patterns — The Breakdown Competitive Analysis

Date: 31 Aug 2026
Source: Live site inspection of 7 competitors

---

## Indian Express Explained

**What they do better:**
- Volume: 20+ explainers published daily, covering breaking events with depth
- Expert Explains series: named subject-matter experts provide authority and trust
- Subscriber Only content creates premium perception and revenue
- "Explained Tracker" section follows developing stories over time
- Trending Topics and category navigation are very strong
- Author bylines prominent on every article

**What they do worse:**
- No evidence chain visible to readers — sources are mentioned but not structured
- No trust scores, confidence ratings, or verification status
- No knowledge graph or entity relationships
- No data tools or interactive visualizations
- Articles are standalone — no connection to broader knowledge systems

**What they don't do:**
- No structured claims with sources
- No evidence scoring or verification pipeline
- No entity terminals or relationship mapping
- No version history or corrections transparency

---

## ISignal (IndiaSpend)

**What they do better:**
- Data-first approach: every story backed by numbers
- Flagship Scheme Tracker: monitors government schemes (MGNREGA, POSHAN, Ujjwala) with longitudinal data
- Multiple named projects: Earthcheck India, Education Check, GenderCheck, Food Price Watch
- DataViz series makes data accessible
- ResearchWire publishes original data analysis
- Strong RTI-based research

**What they do worse:**
- Limited depth per story — data points without connective narrative
- No evidence chain — data is cited but not linked to primary sources
- No entity tracking or relationship mapping
- No trust infrastructure visible to readers

**What they don't do:**
- No claims with confidence scores
- No knowledge graph
- No version history
- No editorial transparency framework

---

## FACTLY

**What they do better:**
- TL;DR format makes data accessible
- Data Dashboards and Counting India tools are genuinely useful
- Fact-check methodology is transparent
- CC-licensed content (open reuse)
- Official source citations are prominent

**What they do worse:**
- Limited depth — most stories are short data summaries
- No knowledge system connecting stories
- No entity tracking
- Weak internal linking

**What they don't do:**
- No evidence chain
- No claim tracking
- No trust infrastructure
- No knowledge graph

---

## Newslaundry

**What they do better:**
- Independent journalism with strong audience ownership (subscription model)
- NL Sena community creates reader loyalty
- Podcast + video + long-form multimedia
- Media criticism (NL Sena, Media Biascope) is unique positioning

**What they do worse:**
- No data or evidence tools
- No knowledge systems
- Paywall limits discoverability
- No structured research infrastructure

---

## The Morning Context

**What they do better:**
- Deep analysis with premium positioning
- Named sections (Business, Chaos, Internet, Startups) create editorial identity
- Corporate subscription model validates B2B demand
- Newsletter by theme allows reader customization
- "State of Unicorns" series shows deep domain expertise

**What they do worse:**
- No data tools or evidence chains
- No knowledge systems connecting stories
- Premium paywall limits reach
- No structured research infrastructure

---

## ThePrint

**What they do better:**
- Broadest coverage: politics, economy, defence, diplomacy, society
- Opinion diversity (multiple columnists with distinct perspectives)
- Ground Reports provide on-the-ground reporting
- "The FinePrint" premium offering validates premium demand
- "50-Word Edit" creates daily digest habit

**What they do worse:**
- No evidence chain or structured sources
- No data tools
- No knowledge systems
- Volume-first approach dilutes depth

---

## Our World in Data

**What they do better:**
- 14,085 charts across 126 topic pages — massive scale
- 29 interactive Data Explorers — genuinely useful tools
- Open access + CC licensing — maximum trust and reuse
- Topic pages are comprehensive research hubs (not just article lists)
- Data Insights (bite-sized updates) create return utility
- Two newsletters serve different reader needs

**What they do worse:**
- India-specific coverage is thin
- No evidence chain for individual claims (different model)
- No entity tracking or relationship mapping
- No editorial transparency framework

## The Breakdown — Position Summary

**Unique advantages (no competitor has these):**
1. Structured evidence chain: Claims → Sources → Evidence → Documents → Timeline
2. Knowledge graph with entity relationships
3. Trust infrastructure: Trust Dashboard, Gold Standard Review, corrections policy
4. Knowledge Library monograph format (15,000+ word evidence-first scholarship)
5. Entity terminals with relationship mapping
6. Confidence scores on every claim
7. Version history and editorial transparency
8. Founding Edition transparency (publishing governance alongside content)

**Weaknesses vs competitors:**
1. Content volume (1 chapter, 9 stories vs Indian Express's 20+ daily)
2. Search quality (naive string matching vs full-text engines)
3. Newsletter (no archive vs ISignal's project-specific newsletters)
4. Author identity (no author pages vs Indian Express's Expert Explains)
5. Data interactivity (static charts vs Our World in Data's 29 explorers)
6. Topic depth (thin topic pages vs ISignal's Flagship Scheme Tracker)

**What The Breakdown can own:**
1. The evidence-first knowledge platform for India
2. Connected knowledge: every article links to entities, claims, evidence, sources, documents, timelines
3. Trust as a product feature (not just a page)
4. Institutional transparency as competitive moat
5. Primary source access and verification
6. Scholarly disagreement surfacing
